# Kasim's Frameworks and Principles

Speaker attribution note: the transcript has no speaker labels. Items marked (Kasim) were clearly said by him in context; others were said by one of the two founders.

| Framework / principle | What it says | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Pareto Talent Framework | In any group of 1,000 people, about 200 generate 80% of output and about 8 of those generate over half | HIRE, via Bea's Day 2 homework [UNVERIFIED here] |
| Charge enough to be extraordinary | Be the premium option (Apple analogy). There will always be someone cheaper, not always someone better. Charging too little means you cannot afford to fix things when they go wrong. | Interview, ch. 11 (Kasim) |
| Traffic first | If you do not know where your traffic is coming from, you do not have a business | Interview, ch. 8 (Kasim) |
| Solve existing problems | Do not assume the problem exists; ask people in your circle. The ones who say they have it are your first customers. | Interview, ch. 8 |
| Buy box | Only serve the client who fits all your parameters, not everyone who has money. When a relationship fails it is usually the client, so manage expectations and say no early. | Interview, ch. 9 |
| Hold your boundaries | Boundaries are yours to uphold; if you soften one, that is your fault. McDonald's cannot make a Caesar salad without underserving everyone else. | Interview, ch. 16 |
| Run through walls | Look for the person who does not stop at the first roadblock and goes around, over or under | Interview, ch. 3 (Kasim) |
| System problem or people problem? | On every failed placement, first ask whether the system is broken; if so, redraw the business map. One unhappy client out of 50 is still a fire to put out. | Interview, ch. 13 |
| Track everything | Keep a database (Airtable or a spreadsheet) of every client, every EA and every transaction from the start, even when you have three clients | Interview, ch. 10 |
| EA as a flywheel | First hire should be an EA to clone yourself; EAs grow into new roles (Kasim's EA became CTO; the next became director of automations; the next director of social) | Interview, ch. 6 (Kasim) |
| Assume partnerships end | "It's not a marriage": plan the divorce before getting married. Jason and John Moran are examples that ended well. | Interview, ch. 4 (Kasim) |
| Value = identifying the work | For as long as the EA is the one prompting the AI, they provide value; if the client has to prompt, AI replaces them | Interview, ch. 15 |
| The 5% rule for tools | About 5% of a tool's functionality makes up 80-90% of real usage, so train EAs on that 5% | Interview, ch. 7 |
| Pay referrals fast | "Pay fast, pay early, pay often"; hunt people down to pay them | Interview, ch. 24 (Kasim) |
| Pay slightly above what is expected | Paid Ivan $1,200 instead of the $1,000 he asked for, to build trust (the book recommends about 10% more) | Interview, ch. 30 |
| Metal detector business | Working with many clients shows you where the opportunities are; new companies come from spotting them | Interview, ch. 29 (Kasim) |
| Stop over-learning | For every hour learning, spend at least an hour doing; get a first paying client from friends and family | Interview, ch. 30 |
| Offer as a workbook | Define guarantee, price and justification by working manually through *$100M Offers* | Interview, ch. 30 |
